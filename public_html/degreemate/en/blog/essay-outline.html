How to write an English essay: one sentence before the first draft

November 11, 2026 / DegreeMate

Many students spend most of their essay time stuck on the first sentence, especially when English isn't their first language. Usually the problem isn't the English. It's that what you want to say hasn't been sorted out yet. Before you write, work through these steps.

1. Mark the verbs in the assignment brief

Verbs like analyze, compare, argue and evaluate tell you what to write. Compare means putting two things side by side on the same criteria. Argue means picking a side and saying so. Note the length, citation style and number of sources while you're at it.

2. Write your argument in one sentence

The center of an English essay is the thesis statement. It has to be a claim, not a topic.

ThesisRemote work raises productivity for routine tasks but lowers it for work that depends on quick feedback.

Once you have that sentence, every body paragraph becomes a reason that supports it. Your first version can change later.

3. Give every paragraph one job

Each body paragraph carries one reason. At the outline stage, notes are enough.

  • Topic sentence: the reason, in one line
  • Evidence: readings, lecture material, examples
  • Explanation: why the evidence supports your claim
  • Link: one line that leads into the next paragraph

4. Draft fast, revise slowly

With an outline in hand, write the draft straight through without stopping for grammar. When you revise, start with the big things. Check that the argument and the paragraph order hold up, then tidy sentences and word choice. Reading aloud makes awkward sentences easy to hear.

Checklist before you submit

  • Is your argument in one sentence at the end of the first paragraph?
  • If you read only the first sentence of each paragraph, does the essay still make sense?
  • Does every source you used have a citation?
  • Did you meet the length and format in the brief?

A degree plan that starts from what you've already done

Often the quickest way to finish is to get credit for the coursework and work experience you already have.

Routes we look at

U.S. degrees

If you're enrolled at a U.S. school or considering a U.S. online degree

  • Transfer credit: how much of your old coursework each school will accept
  • Credit by exam: CLEP, DSST and similar tests for general-education credits
  • Prior Learning Assessment: credit for work experience through a portfolio
  • Competency-based programs, where you move on by passing assessments rather than waiting out a term

Every school sets its own limits. We always confirm the final answer with the school's registrar.

Korean degrees

If you're working in Korea and want to finish a bachelor's there

  • Credit Bank System: 140 credits for a bachelor's, with up to 42 coursework credits recognised a year
  • Certificate and self-study degree credits count separately from that yearly cap
  • Bachelor's Degree Examination for Self-Education: a degree earned through staged exams
  • Keeping track of degree application windows so you don't miss one

Source: National Institute for Lifelong Education, Credit Bank guide (cb.or.kr), as of November 2026. Rules can change.

How a planning session runs

  1. Gather records

    Send transcripts, certificates and a work history. Unofficial transcripts are fine.

  2. Credit audit

    We sort your credits into likely to count and needs checking.

  3. Compare routes

    On a 60-minute call we lay out each route's time and cost side by side.

  4. Action plan

    You get a report with deadlines, exam dates and documents in date order.

Fifteen minutes is enough to sketch the rest of your degree.
